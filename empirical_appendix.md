# Empirical appendix: distinguishable prediction and policy estimand

**Status:** proposed and unregistered. No organization, data agreement, treatment assignment, or experiment exists. This appendix is separate from the completed theoretical results. It supersedes the earlier longer empirical protocol for this revision.

## 1. Target and feasible comparison

Preferred setting: repeated internal funding cohorts with an intake registry and independently measurable domain-level costs and benefits. The observation frame is all teams eligible before any rule is announced, including teams that never apply. The assignment unit is a persistent committee-domain cluster, not an individual proposal. A team must not move between arms to seek a different standard.

The distinctive prediction is Theorem 2, not simply lower funding for poorly measurable projects. Compare an optimized common statistical cutoff with independently calibrated type-specific cutoffs at the same allocated capacity. Compare those rule effects across two ex ante scarcity strata on opposite sides of an estimated crossing. If capacity itself can be varied with separate operational authorization, a factorial design can randomize budget and rule jointly. Within each budget level, both rules receive exactly the same budget and training resources. The current task does not authorize implementation.

A lower alpha means greater verification power at a fixed low-quality false-positive probability. Alpha must be calibrated from pre-policy validation work, not assigned from eventual success. Private benefit R and the cost elasticity eta are also model primitives, not labels an analyst can read directly from financial returns. Unless these quantities and the applicable verification family are credible, test the general marginal-frontier condition rather than the exact exponential crossing formula.

Record score distributions and decision compliance separately: more data possession is not proof that the approval rule used them. Measure application costs for unfunded applicants as well as winners. Quality is a latent construct; a negative realized payoff is not automatically a false ex ante approval.

## 3. Estimand, inference, and economic outcomes

For a fixed follow-up horizon H, let V_c(r,K) be the discounted domain-level net value generated by the entire policy path, including application labor, implementation, optimal or actual abandonment, follow-on costs, and displaced ordinary operations. The primary contrast at capacity K is

Delta_H(K) = E[V_c(type-specific,K) - V_c(common,K)].

Keep clusters with no submissions in the denominator. Where project attribution is contentious, use an independently measured domain-level outcome and reconcile it to the project ledger. Blinded valuation and fixed accounting rules are essential; randomization does not cure treatment-dependent project valuation. Separate observed cash flows through H from terminal-value assumptions. No short-run outcome is automatically lifetime innovation value.

For a prospective design, a single primary outcome is net portfolio value at the predeclared horizon; the mechanism family consists of the rule-by-scarcity interaction in class-specific good-project funding and independently rated proposal composition. Use randomization inference respecting blocks and persistent clusters, with Holm adjustment within the secondary mechanism family. Report policy effects even if the parametric mechanism fails. Do not treat estimated project types or post-treatment adherence as randomized quantities.

Sufficient power requires independent assignment clusters. Under balanced assignment and standardized independent cluster outcomes, 80% power at two-sided 5% size requires an effect of about 5.60/sqrt(number of clusters) SD. This is a planning approximation, not a power calculation based on real data. Ten or twelve committees cannot identify modest interactions merely by collecting many correlated proposals. Pilot variance, cost, and long-run follow-up feasibility remain unknown.

## 4. Selection, missing labels, interference, and competing explanations

A randomized policy comparison can identify its whole-domain value effect without observing the funded potential payoff of every rejected proposal, provided the actual relevant policy-induced accounting stream is observed. Unfunded projects can still incur proposal costs and create alternative-funding or spillover effects. A zero internal implementation receipt is not a zero potential funded payoff.

Missing funded outcomes for rejected proposals obstruct individual false-rejection costs and oracle efficiency rankings. A separately authorized funding lottery can identify some marginal funded potential outcomes; it is not required for every policy-level causal contrast. Follow-up on a rejected project funded elsewhere is an alternative treatment outcome. Public SBIR awards and NSF award abstracts do not contain the required rule randomization, full intake population, or internal value accounting and cannot identify this intervention.

Potential confounds in the mechanism test include class-specific cash-flow horizons, intrinsic risk, skills, political influence, and score manipulation. Unequal pilot learning or capital recycling invalidates the common-continuation comparison. Map shared staff and idea spillovers before assignment; randomize at a sufficiently large unit or state partial-interference assumptions. Repeated rounds within a committee do not create independent clusters if the same people learn across policies.

Bounds for incomplete policy outcomes require externally defensible support. If an arm has weighted missing share p and outcomes in [L,U], its mean lies between (1-p) times the observed mean plus pL and the same expression with pU. Subtract arm-specific bounds for a contrast. Without credible finite support, do not report artificially narrow welfare bounds.

## 5. Falsification and feasibility

The exact model is weakened if no common normalized null score is credible, if application costs depend strongly on quality, if private funding benefits vary substantially by class, if entry occurs after applicants learn the score, or if institutional commitment is absent. Those failures need a different model, not relabeling alpha. A relaxation that increases hard-to-measure applications without higher net value does not validate a welfare improvement. Better funded quality alongside higher application cost can lower net value, as Proposition 4 shows.

Immediate feasibility assessment: the theory and synthetic verification are complete. A causal organizational test is not currently feasible from the supplied files. It needs a partner, complete intake and value records, validation of the score technology, sufficient independent clusters, and separate authorization. No public award file closes those gaps on its own.
